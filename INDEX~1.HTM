Sources & assumptions

  • Default mortgage rate 6.66% — Freddie Mac PMMS 30-year fixed average, week of 07/30/2026.
  • Default wedding budget $34,200 and 117-guest average — The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study (surveyed 10,474 US couples married in 2025). The median is meaningfully lower than the mean, so treat $34.2k as an upper-middle benchmark, not a target.
  • Wedding category percentages are national-average allocations, not quotes. Replace every line with a real number as you get it.
  • Property tax rate, insurance, and HOA are inputs with no meaningful national default — look up your specific county and get an actual insurance quote.
  • The model holds property taxes and insurance flat. In reality both trend up roughly 2–4%/yr, and insurance has risen much faster than that in several states. The stress-test panel covers this.
  • No home-price appreciation is assumed, so PMI drop-off is driven by principal paydown alone. That is deliberately conservative — appreciation or an appraisal can remove PMI sooner.
  • Closing costs default to 3% of purchase price. Real range is roughly 2–5% depending on state, loan type, and whether you buy points.
  • The effective tax rate input should bundle federal, state, FICA, 401(k), and health premiums. Easiest way to get it right: take a pay stub and compute 1 − (net pay ÷ gross pay).

How to use this

  1. Fill in the Living tab income section first — every ratio depends on it.
  2. On House, drag price and down payment until the front-end ratio and monthly surplus both look survivable. That's your real budget, not what a lender pre-approves you for.
  3. Replace living-cost placeholders with three months of your actual statements. Placeholder budgets are always too optimistic.
  4. On Wedding, set the ceiling first, then let the tracker tell you when quotes push you past it.
  5. Check Timeline for a shortfall. If the line dips below zero, one of the two dates has to move.
  6. Save to file writes a .json snapshot. Keep it next to the spreadsheet; load it to pick up where you left off.

Getting your numbers onto another device, or to your partner

Click Share in the top right. Everything you have changed is packed into a short code, and the address bar keeps it up to date as you edit — so bookmarking the page bookmarks your scenario.

  • Scenario code — the option that works today, with no hosting. Copy it, text or email it, and the other person pastes it into box 3 of their own copy of this file. They see your exact numbers.
  • Share link — the full URL with the code attached. If you are opening this file from your hard drive the link only works on that machine, because the path points at your disk. Put the file on any free static host (Cloudflare Pages, GitHub Pages, Netlify) and the link starts working everywhere.
  • Nothing is uploaded. There is no server, no account, and no database behind this page. The code is your data, compressed into text. That also means a link posted somewhere public exposes your income and savings, so treat it like a private document.
  • For editing at the same time, use the spreadsheet on Google Sheets. Two people typing into this app will overwrite each other, because each browser holds its own copy.

This is a planning model, not financial advice. Confirm every figure with your lender, insurer, and tax authority before committing to anything.
